C. V. Raman, "On the Wolf-Note in Bowed String Instruments," Philosophical Magazine, Series 6, Vol. 32, pp. 391–395 (1916).

This is the paper that first treated the wolf-note phenomenon as what's essentially a two-coupled-oscillator problem — string mode interacting with a high-Q body resonance, energy sloshing back and forth between them near the coincidence frequency. It's distinct from Raman's other well-known 1918 bowed-string paper ("On the Mechanical Theory of the Vibrations of Bowed Strings," Bulletin of the Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science), which deals with Helmholtz-motion string dynamics generally rather than string–body coupling specifically — worth not conflating the two since they're often shelved together.

J. C. Schelleng, "The Violin as a Circuit," Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 35(3), 326–338 (1963).

Schelleng's contribution reformulates the coupling using an electrical-circuit/impedance analogy rather than an explicit oscillator-pair formalism, but it's the paper that made the coupled string-body picture rigorous and quantitative for a general audience, and most modern wolf-note/coupled-oscillator papers cite Raman (1916) and Schelleng (1963) together as the two-part foundation.

C. Gough, "Violin Acoustics: The Acoustics of Thin-Walled Shallow Boxes — A Tale of Coupled Oscillators," Acoustics Today 12(2), 22–30 (2016)
 
  — and the more accessible. the latter is explicitly framed around the coupled-oscillator picture and is a much easier read than the 1916/1963 originals if you're looking to actually apply the formalism to your composite build.
